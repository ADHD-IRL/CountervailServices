The problem

Adversarial testing is bounded by three limits. AI attacks all three at once.

  1. 01

    Bandwidth

    Skilled red teamers and exercise designers are scarce. Every scenario they build is one they can't build elsewhere.

  2. 02

    Imagination

    Teams inherit the same mental models as the defenders they test. Groupthink and familiarity bias shrink the threat space.

  3. 03

    Cadence

    Exercises are costly, so they're rare. Findings go stale as systems, threats, and people change.

Traditional red teaming is a scheduled sparring match with one opponent. AI-augmented red teaming is a gym full of tireless sparring partners, each fighting in a different style. The human coach still decides what the fighter needs to work on.

Operating principle

AI proposes. Humans dispose.

Any action that touches production, is irreversible, or affects people requires explicit human authorization. Agents run on least privilege, and every tool call is logged. Humans hold the decision gates at scoping, authorization of active testing, adjudication of findings, and acceptance of risk.

Signature methods

Approaches that weren't practical before AI.

Beyond doing existing work faster, these methods were previously out of reach because of cost, staffing, or cognitive load.

01

Adversarial Multi-Agent Debate

Red, blue, and judge agents argue a scenario through multiple rounds. Like a moot court for security, the value lies in the arguments the judge can't dismiss. Your experts review the surviving arguments instead of generating them from scratch.

02

The Thousand Pre-Mortems

Hundreds of independent pre-mortems, each seeded with a different persona, threat actor, or failure. Clustered results turn intuition into something closer to a statistical distribution of failure.

03

Persona-Based SME Councils

Engineered AI SMEs review a plan from their lens, with a required dissent step against consensus.

04

Digital Twin Wargaming

Agents run attack and failure scenarios against a mirror of production, continuously, with no risk to operations.

05

Change-Triggered Exercises

A focused micro-exercise fires when a system goes live, a key person leaves, a vendor changes, or a new threat report drops.

06

Disposable, Mission-Specific Tooling

Build exactly the tool the scenario needs, then throw it away. No more bending generic tools to fit.

07

Red Teaming the AI Itself

Prompt injection via documents and web content, compromised MCP servers, excessive agent permissions, exfiltration through outputs.

08

Plan Assassination

One objective: find the single assumption that, if false, collapses the plan. Iterate until the plan survives.

AI SME Council

More perspectives in every room.

An AI SME is an engineered persona: a defined role, a grounding corpus of authoritative sources, an analytic standard such as ICD 203 tradecraft, and explicit limits on what it may assert. It behaves less like a chatbot and more like a structured analytic instrument.

Think of it as a reference librarian who has read the entire specialty library and can argue from it, but has never been in the field. Excellent for breadth and structured critique. No substitute for lived experience.

  • Threat Actor Emulator

    Thinks and plans as a specific adversary; challenges defensive assumptions.

    Threat intel · ATT&CK groups · historical campaigns

  • Mission Owner

    Evaluates impact to mission outcomes and operational tempo.

    CONOPS · mission essential functions · BIA

  • Detection Engineer

    Assesses whether activity would be seen and how to detect it.

    Detection rules · log schemas · D3FEND

  • Compliance Assessor

    Tests findings against control frameworks and assessment objectives.

    NIST 800-53 · 800-171 · CMMC guides

  • Systems Safety Engineer

    Examines hazards, failure modes, and cascading effects.

    FMEA · STPA · safety standards

  • Insider Threat Analyst

    Explores abuse of legitimate access and human factors.

    Insider threat indicators · access policy

  • Legal & Public Affairs

    Surfaces disclosure, regulatory, and reputational consequences.

    Breach notification rules · comms plans

  • Contrarian / Devil's Advocate

    Required to argue against consensus in every review.

    The plan itself and all prior outputs

How we keep AI SMEs credible

Grounding over recall
Factual claims cite the grounding corpus.
Calibrated confidence
Explicit confidence levels; evidence kept separate from inference.
Structured dissent
At least one opposing view per review, to counter model agreeableness.
Bounded authority
AI SMEs advise. They never approve, authorize, or accept risk.
Model diversity
Personas run on different models to reduce correlated blind spots.
Calibrated before use
Tested against golden scenarios and human SMEs, like a new analyst on probation.

Reference operating model

Seven phases. Human gates where they matter.

  1. 1

    Scope & objectives

    AI drafts scope, maps mission to assets, proposes threat actors.

    Human approves scope and rules of engagement.

  2. 2

    Intelligence & context

    AI summarizes threat intel and maps TTPs to ATT&CK.

    Human validates relevance and sourcing.

  3. 3

    Hypothesis generation

    AI generates and ranks attack and failure hypotheses.

    Human curates and prioritizes.

  4. 4

    Scenario & tool build

    AI writes scenarios, injects, and tooling.

    Human reviews code and scenario realism.

  5. 5

    Execution

    AI runs approved tests in a range or twin; plays TTX roles.

    Human authorizes actions and holds the kill switch.

  6. 6

    Analysis

    AI correlates evidence, drafts findings and AAR.

    Human adjudicates findings and severity.

  7. 7

    Remediation & learning

    AI drafts detections, fixes, and register updates.

    Human accepts risk and assigns owners.

Governance, safety & guardrails

Governed like any other offensive capability.

Aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, applicable DoD and agency AI policy, and your existing rules of engagement for offensive testing.

Agent exceeds scope

Machine-readable rules of engagement, allow-listed targets, least-privilege MCP credentials.

Production impact

Human approval gates, sandbox or twin execution by default, and a kill switch.

Sensitive data exposure

Classification enforcement; local or accredited models (FedRAMP High, DoD IL4/IL5) for CUI and above.

Compromised MCP servers

Vetted server inventory, code review, pinned versions, network isolation.

Prompt injection

All retrieved content treated as untrusted data; instructions separated from data; output filtering.

Unverified findings

Mandatory human adjudication and evidence traceability before anything is reported.

Auditability gaps

Full logging of prompts, tool calls, outputs, and approvals.

Over-reliance on AI

Periodic human-only exercises to maintain team skill and benchmark AI value.
